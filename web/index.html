Three choices make it fast.

A check that runs twice is paid for twice. Forge is arranged so that a collision is found before the check, and the check runs once.

GitHub flow, when two agents overlap
  1. Branchthe agent works
  2. Pull requestopened
  3. CIruns
  4. Conflictfound after CI
  5. Rebasethe agent redoes its part
  6. CI againon the new base
  7. main
Forge, same overlap
  1. Lanethe agent works
  2. Syncoverlap reported here
  3. Landjoins the queue
  4. One checkon the exact result
  5. main

  1. Overlap is reported while the agents are still working.

    Every save is compared with the other Lanes. Two agents that touch the same lines both hear it then, not after CI has run.

  2. One check, on the exact result.

    Forge runs your CI on the change as it will sit on main, on top of everything landing before it. What passed is what main becomes.

    9 CI jobs against 29 on the release run.

  3. A queue, not a pile of pull requests.

    Finished work lands in order. The next Lane is checked on top of the one before it, so nobody rebases to find out where main is.

What the benchmark does not show

  • One repository, three runs per setting. Too few to claim the same result on yours.
  • Two series. The ten-agent figures come from a later series than those for 1, 3 and 5 agents.
  • Forge was worse on one measure. With ten agents its agents took 13 recovery turns against 7.
  • The release build was closer. One later run: 375 s against 474 s to main, and 6 of ten tasks strictly right against 7.
  • Outcomes, not causes. The report describes what happened. It does not measure how much each design choice contributes, and it is not a pass of the exit bar it was run for.

Method, raw runs and every table

Do I have to leave GitHub?

No. Import copies your repository and Forge mirrors main back after every landing. Issues, releases and Actions stay where they are.

Does Forge run my agents or my tests?

Neither. Agents run where they run today. Tests run on your CI, with your secrets, on your bill. Forge decides what is checked together and what moves main.

Who approves a landing?

You choose per project. Checked work can land on its own, or wait for a person in Needs you. Paths you protect always wait for a person.

What if an agent lands something wrong?

Take it out of main with one action. That is a new landing that removes exactly that change and leaves everything after it alone.

Will the benchmark hold for my repository?

It may not. Forge records time to main and check minutes for every landing, so you can compare on your own work.

Can I use it today?

Forge is in open development and builds itself. There is no public service or package yet, and nothing is charged. Development status
